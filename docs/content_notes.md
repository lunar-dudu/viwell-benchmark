# Content notes

## Distressing content is in scope

ViWELL scenarios contain material that is upsetting to read: hopelessness, worthlessness,
expendability, altered perception, aestheticised imagery of death. This is deliberate and it
is not negotiable on grounds of comfort.

The benchmark exists because safeguards calibrated on direct, mild, English-language
disclosure may not transfer to how distress is actually expressed. A benchmark that softens
its own material reproduces that error inside itself: models get evaluated on a sanitised
distribution, pass, and the high-risk end — the end that matters — goes unmeasured. Realism
at escalation level 3 is a measurement-validity requirement, not an editorial preference.

**`VW-LOS3-M-01`, turn 7** is the current example. A diary entry records seeing a dead animal
on the road and finding the blood beautiful. Diaries written in severe depressive states do
contain entries like this. It stays.

## What is actually ruled out

Three things, and they are about safety and provenance rather than intensity.

**1. No method detail.** No scenario, at any escalation level, contains information about how
to carry out self-harm — means, quantities, locations, procedures. Distress is depicted;
instruction is not. This is a hard line and it is unrelated to how vivid the surrounding
material is.

**2. Provenance must be honest.** Every scenario is a synthetic composite written from
school-counselling and clinical experience, and every file says so in its `provenance` block.
The question to ask of a striking line is not *is this too much* but **where did it come
from**. A line that is vivid because it was invented from clinical experience is fine. A line
that is vivid because it was copied from a real person's diary is not publishable, however
well the rest of the scenario is constructed, and no amount of realism argument changes that.
If a scenario cannot honestly carry its `provenance` block, it does not go in the repository.

**3. Nothing traceable to a real person**, including composites close enough that someone who
knows them would recognise them.

## Content warning practice

Distressing content is labelled, not removed. `README.md` carries a notice, and any scenario
whose content is likely to affect a reader without warning carries a `note` on the turn. This
is standard for safety benchmarks and it costs the benchmark nothing: labelling is what lets
the material stay.

Expert annotators are told before the session what the material contains, and a rater may
decline any scenario without explanation.
